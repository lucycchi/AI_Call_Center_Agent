# Findings: Why Meridian Home Comfort's Customers Call

What the 420 call transcripts (July to September 2026) show about why customers call, which calls the agent handles, how well staff handle them today, and three findings for the executive team. Generated by `python -m pipeline.findings` from the labeled data in `data/`.

## In short

- **Top five reasons, by calls:** new booking 32%; billing 18%; membership 17%; warranty 11%; existing appointment 9%. Together they are 87% of calls.
- **Scope:** the agent handles bookings, existing appointments and warranty, and the parts of billing and membership that documents or a read-only lookup can answer. It hands off anything that needs a human's authority or an action on money or membership, and every emergency after a fixed safety reply.
- **Staff today** resolve 85% of calls on the call, or 73% leaving out calls only an account action resolves; the second figure is the fair comparison for the agent.
- **For the executive team:** 26% of calls contain a staff policy error, the north zone has 70% of customers but 31% of bookable slots, and the documents are missing, stale or contradicted on autopay, waitlists and the diagnostic credit.

## Method

- **The transcripts are templated.** The 420 calls fall into 52 templates: calls that share the same request and the same staff outcome once names, numbers and filler words are removed. Labels are given per template. Nine waitlist templates are the matching booking call plus one fixed waitlist exchange; each is linked to its booking twin as a family (43 families), and families stay on one side of the test split (template families (D39)).
- **Labels.** Claude Haiku 5.5 labeled every call against a fixed category list. Codex (gpt-6.1-sol, OpenAI) labeled one sample call per template without seeing Haiku's labels; these Codex labels are the reference. Claude Fable 5.1 labeled a blind third of the templates as a cross-company check.
- **Agreement.** Codex against Haiku: 100% of templates. Fable against Codex on the blind third (17 templates): 100% on the call reason and 100% on the sub-reason. No human labeled the templates; the agreement is between models from two companies.
- **Why agreement is perfect.** It reflects how clean and repetitive these synthetic transcripts are, and should not be read as evidence that real calls are this easy to sort.
- **Volume** is ranked by number of calls; staff minutes come from the call durations in the transcript index.

## Human baseline

- Staff resolved 355 of 420 calls on the call (85%).
- Excluding calls resolved only by an account action the agent will not take (payment links, autopay, cancelling auto-renew, upgrades): 308 (73%). This is the fair comparison for the agent's containment.
- In 11 calls the caller asked to book and staff ended the call without booking.
- These figures count waitlist calls (the caller was offered a waitlist and still booked) and dropped bookings as resolved. Excluding the dropped bookings, staff resolved 344 (82%).

## Executive findings

### Staff policy errors

108 of 420 calls (26%) contain a staff statement or action that contradicts the current documents:

- offered a waitlist: 34 calls
- booked a burning smell as a normal visit: 16 calls
- promised a manager callback today: 16 calls
- moved a next-day appointment without the $49 fee: 15 calls
- explained an invoice as the diagnostic fee plus the repair: 13 calls
- dropped a booking the caller asked for: 11 calls
- promised a refund callback in two business days: 8 calls

Errors appear for every staff member, so they are systemic (training and documentation), not one person.

### Capacity imbalance by zone

| Zone | Customers | Bookable slots | HVAC repair | Tune-up | Plumbing | Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| north | 108 | 114 | 54 | 26 | 0 | 34 |
| central | 15 | 145 | 52 | 19 | 74 | 0 |
| south | 31 | 106 | 29 | 7 | 40 | 30 |

The north zone has 70% of customers but 31% of bookable slots and no plumbing slots; the central zone has 10% of customers but 40% of slots and no estimate slots. Rebalancing technicians would cut wait times more than any agent.

### Documentation gaps

- **Autopay:** 14 calls ask about it; no document covers it.
- **Waitlist:** staff offered a waitlist in 34 calls; only a stale 2024 guide mentions one and the scheduling database has none.
- **Diagnostic credit:** 45 of 81 invoices charge the $89 diagnostic fee on top of a repair, although the FAQ says it is credited toward an approved repair, and 22 charge it to customers who are members today, for whom it is waived (the invoices do not record the tier at the time).

The seeded scheduling database also has 113 existing appointments outside the customer's zone; they are listed in the appendix at the end.

## Glossary

- **Call reason (call driver):** why a customer called.
- **Template:** a call pattern repeated across many transcripts with only names, numbers and filler words changed.
- **Reference label:** the label treated as correct when measuring; here Codex's label for each template.
- **Blind label:** a label given without seeing anyone else's label for the same item.
- **Human baseline:** the share of calls staff resolved during the call.
- **Action gap:** calls the agent understands but hands off because they need an action on money or membership.
- **Bookable slot:** an appointment slot that is open, or reserved for members.
- **Zone:** the area (north, central, south) a customer's city belongs to.
